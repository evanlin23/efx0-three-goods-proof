# Novelty of TARGET and conjecture D: literature search

Workstream `proof/lit-search`, 2026-09-25. Search log (every query and source, with access notes): `results/lit_search_log.md`. Papers read here are also recorded in `proofs/citations.md` ("Other papers checked"). No ledger status is changed by this file.

Read levels: **full** (whole text read), **section** (named pages or sections read), **grep** (full text searched by pattern, every match read in context), **abstract**, **snippet** (search result or metadata only), **earlier** (read in full in an earlier session, recorded in `proofs/citations.md`, not re-read here). Anything not read at one of these levels is marked [unverified].

The claims checked:
- **TARGET**: every additive instance with nonnegative values in which each agent positively values at most 3 goods has a complete EFX₀ allocation (v_i(X_i) ≥ v_i(X_j ∖ {g}) for all i ≠ j and all g ∈ X_j, including goods i values at 0).
- **D**: if every agent values exactly 3 goods and is balanced, some EFX₀ allocation has at most one bundle with more than two goods. *Balanced* means 2·v_i(g) ≤ v_i(M) for every good g: no good is worth more to agent i than its other two relevant goods combined (a ≤ b + c).

## 1. Verdict

**Novel.** No published paper or preprint found proves TARGET, D, or anything that implies either. Confidence: high for TARGET (about 90 %), higher for D (about 95 %), since D's shape statement has no counterpart anywhere in the literature.

Why nothing found implies TARGET:
- **Viswanathan–Mehta** (AAMAS 2024, 3-page extended abstract; read in full) is the only work on few relevant goods per agent. Its first author, Maya Viswanathan, was then at the University of Illinois Laboratory High School, Urbana (p. 2534), a 2023–24 mentee of Ruta Mehta (Mehta's CV), so no thesis version exists to look for. It announces, with a proof sketch, **ordinary EFX** (only goods the envier values positively may be removed) for agents with at most **4** relevant goods. Footnote 2 describes (without naming) the stronger EFX₀ notion ("Many works consider a stronger version"), and its algorithm ends by giving the goods nobody values "to an arbitrary agent" (p. 2), a step that is harmless for EFX but can break EFX₀. No full version was found: none on arXiv, none in Ruta Mehta's 2026 CV (which lists it only as AAMAS 2024 paper C51), and the two NSF-PAR copies are the same extended abstract. It has no indexed citations (Google Scholar shows no "Cited by"; Crossref and OpenAlex count 0), and none of the 165 arXiv papers from April 2024 on that the searches returned cites it (full-text search, log §4). TARGET and this result are **incomparable**: TARGET has the stronger notion, Viswanathan–Mehta the larger bound. TARGET implies their result for agents with at most three relevant goods, since EFX₀ implies EFX; theirs does not imply TARGET.
- The other existence results that contain parts of TARGET restrict something TARGET leaves free: the number of agents (n ≤ 3: Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn; n = 4 with m ≤ 9: Alkassar–Fouz–Mehlhorn), the number of goods (m ≤ n + 3: Mahara; exactly m = n + 4: Bratby's unrefereed, AI-generated preprint of 2026-09-08, see below), the number of valuers per good (at most two: the multigraph theorems), the hypergraph girth (≥ 4: Lianeas–Sgouritsa–Sotiriou), the number of valuation types (at most three), or the values themselves (binary, bivalued). A concrete instance outside all of them is the connected core with six agents whose relevant sets are {0, 1, 6}, {0, 1, 7}, {0, 2, 8}, {2, 3, 4}, {3, 5, 9}, {4, 5, 10}, with pairwise distinct values drawn from the interval (1, 2), which makes every agent balanced. It has n = 6 and m = 11 = n + 5, so neither Mahara (m ≤ n + 3) nor Bratby (m = n + 4) applies. Good 0 is valued by three agents, so it is not a multigraph instance. Agents 0 and 1 share goods 0 and 1, a 2-cycle in the hypergraph where goods are hyperedges on agents, so the girth is 2. Each agent has three distinct positive values, so the valuations are neither binary nor bivalued nor ternary, and all six differ, so there are more than three types. TARGET covers this instance; no earlier theorem found covers it, refereed or not. (The core of PROMPT.md §3, with n = 6 and m = 10 = n + 4, used here before, is covered by Bratby's preprint if that preprint is correct.)
- TARGET would follow from the additive EFX conjecture, via the perturbation lemma L1 (Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn's Lemma 1 is the same argument). That conjecture is still open for n ≥ 4: EconCSLib states it as an open problem (2606.16144, Fig. 5, p. 7), and Wang (2608.30203) and Alkassar–Fouz–Mehlhorn call it open.
- **Bratby (Zenodo, 2026-09-08; missed in the first pass, flagged by the coordinator's review).** J. Bratby, "Complete EFX Allocations with Four More Goods than Agents", claims complete EFX₀ for every additive nonnegative instance with exactly m = n + 4 (Theorem 1.1, p. 5: "The deletion quantifier includes goods g with v_i(g) = 0"). Its front matter (p. 1) says GPT-6 Astra did substantial parts of the proof construction and internal verification, and that the proof has had no independent human peer review. It is unrefereed and AI-generated, and was not checked here (149 pages). It cannot imply TARGET, since TARGET puts no bound on m (a core can have up to 2n goods). Its §1.3 (p. 6), a survey of related existence results, mentions neither Viswanathan–Mehta nor few relevant goods. **It changes neither the verdict nor the confidence**; it only removed the old showcase example (above). It does show that the first pass missed a general-purpose repository. A Zenodo search (log §6) finds no other EFX preprint there.

**Counter-evidence (question 4): none.** No claimed counterexample to EFX₀ (or EFX) for additive valuations exists in anything found. The known non-existence results are for monotone, submodular or subadditive valuations (2604.18216, 2605.06451), for chores (Christoforidis–Santorinaios 2406.10752, superadditive costs; He–Tao 2606.08872, additive costs; Lin et al. 2608.10572; abstracts), and for EFX *orientations* on graphs (Christodoulou et al., Fig. 1). None of them applies to additive goods.

**Residual risks.**
1. *Work not visible to the searches.* The searches cover arXiv (92 queries, 290 papers, full texts of the 165 from April 2024 on), the open web (22 queries), Zenodo (API), Google Scholar (title level), Crossref, and the EC 2026 program. Proceedings-only 2025–2026 papers, theses, course notes and preprints on general-purpose repositories could be missed. The first pass missed Bratby's Zenodo preprint; the second pass searched Zenodo (API) and, through the web-search tool, ResearchGate, SSRN, OSF, HAL, preprints.org, TechRxiv and Optimization Online, and found nothing further. One unread item is on record: EC 2026 paper #136, "EFX allocations on multigraphs" (Christodoulou, Mastrakouis, Sgouritsa, Sotiriou), which is not on arXiv [unverified]. Its title restricts it to multigraphs, where each good has at most two valuers, so it cannot imply TARGET unless its content goes beyond its title.
2. *Citation lists.* Semantic Scholar was unreachable (HTTP 429) and OpenAlex over budget, so forward citations of Viswanathan–Mehta were checked only through Google Scholar (none), Crossref and OpenAlex counts (0), and the arXiv full-text sweep (none). A non-arXiv citing paper could be missed.
3. *Unpublished full version.* Viswanathan–Mehta's extended abstract gives only a proof sketch. An unpublished full version could handle EFX₀; nothing indicates it does. Ruta Mehta, the second author, is the natural person to ask.
4. *Folklore.* The k = 2 case (serial dictatorship; L2c, and the owner's evanlin23/mrd-efx) is elementary and may be folklore. Nothing found states k = 3 in any form.
5. *Later preprints.* This search ends on 2026-09-25.

## 4a. (p, q)-bounded instances (added 2026-09-25, workstream `proof/lit-pq`)
The (p, q)-bounded papers are now read: in full 2407.05139, 2506.09288, CFKS and 2502.09777; at section level 2406.12413. Their relation to this project is in `proofs/pq_bounded.md`. The summary:
- In the agent–good incidence graph, k bounds agent degrees and p bounds good degrees; q bounds how many goods two agents share. So k ≤ 3 implies (∞, 3)-bounded.
- Among results that restrict only *which agents value which goods*, exact EFX₀ is known only for p ≤ 2 (any q) and for hypergraphs of girth ≥ 4. Every one we read with p ≥ 3 assumes girth ≥ 4 (2608.03171); those read only from abstracts all have p ≤ 2. For q = 1 in general only EF2X, partial or approximate results exist.
- So nothing there implies TARGET or D. Read in (p, q) terms, TARGET is exact EFX₀ on agent degree ≤ 3 for every p and q, with no girth or multiplicity condition. Among the structural results, every earlier exact one with p ≥ 3 needs hypergraph girth ≥ 4. Results that bound n, m, the number of valuation types or the values (§1–§2) do allow p ≥ 3, and so does T5.
- In the (k, p) table, the first open cell is (k, p) = (4, 3). The H_t family that defeats bounded-rotation LB₄ʳ has p = 4 and q = 1.
- Four of K4.MC7's six graphical cores are covered by older theorems (CFKS for the two simple ones, bipartite-multigraph theorems for three). Only two need the general multigraph theorem.

## 5. Recommended wording (research proposal)

Suggested text:

> We prove that every fair-division instance with nonnegative additive valuations in which each agent positively values at most three goods admits a complete EFX₀ allocation (envy-freeness up to any good, where the removed good may be worthless to the envious agent). Moreover, when every agent values exactly three goods and is balanced (2·v_i(g) ≤ v_i(M) for every good g, i.e. no good is worth more than the agent's other two combined), some EFX₀ allocation has at most one bundle of more than two goods. To our knowledge both results are new. The closest prior result, by Viswanathan and Mehta (AAMAS 2024, extended abstract), announces, with a proof sketch, that ordinary EFX allocations exist when each agent values at most four goods. Their notion allows removing only goods the envious agent values positively, and their algorithm gives unvalued goods to an arbitrary agent, so it does not establish EFX₀; the two results are incomparable. Previously, EFX₀ was known on this class only in special cases, among them at most three agents (Chaudhury, Garg and Mehlhorn), four agents and at most nine goods (Alkassar, Fouz and Mehlhorn), at most n + 3 goods (Mahara), goods valued by at most two agents (the graph and multigraph theorems of Christodoulou et al. and Afshinmehr et al.), hypergraphs of girth at least four (Lianeas, Sgouritsa and Sotiriou), and, in an unrefereed preprint, exactly n + 4 goods (Bratby 2026). Both theorems are machine-checked in Lean 4 (core Lean, standard axioms only), for values in any linearly ordered cancellative commutative monoid, such as ℕ, ℚ≥0 or ℝ≥0. That ℚ≥0 and ℝ≥0 are such monoids is a standard fact, not a Lean theorem; ℕ is a Lean instance.

Wording to avoid:
- "the first machine-checked proof of an EFX existence theorem." EconCSLib (two agents) and Akrami et al. (the encoding behind their n = 3, m = 7 result) come first. If a formalization claim is wanted: "to our knowledge, the first machine-checked EFX₀ existence theorem for an unbounded number of agents, apart from our earlier two-goods development."
- Citing Bratby's m = n + 4 preprint as an established theorem. Write "an unrefereed preprint claims".
- "strengthens" or "extends" Viswanathan–Mehta. The results are incomparable (stronger notion, smaller bound).
- "resolves an open problem." Nothing found poses the three-goods EFX₀ question in print.
- "novel techniques" for the peeling rule R1, the envy-graph source placement, or the perturbation lemma. These have prior art (§4). The core reduction as a whole, the ordinal case analysis, LB⁺'s need-chain rotation with the hitting-set owner test, and the specific local search can be presented as new, with "to our knowledge".
- "machine-checked over the reals" without the qualifier. Since PR #20 the Lean statements (`EFX.target_ordered`, `EFX.corollaryD_ordered`) hold over any `EFX.OrderedValue`. That ℝ≥0 is such a type is the standard fact the ledger relies on, not a Lean theorem (Lean core has no reals). Say "for any linearly ordered cancellative commutative monoid, such as ℕ, ℚ≥0 or ℝ≥0". This is the `EFX.OrderedValue` class of `lean/EFX/RealValues.lean`: a commutative monoid with a total order in which a ≤ b ⟺ a + c ≤ b + c.
